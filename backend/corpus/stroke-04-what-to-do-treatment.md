# What to Do If You Suspect a Stroke

A stroke needs urgent medical help in hospital because it can be life-threatening. It happens when blood stops flowing properly to part of the brain, and every minute that passes without treatment means more brain cells are damaged or die. This is why quick recognition and immediate action matter so much.

## Step 1: Recognize and Call for Help

Use the FAST test — Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services. If you notice any one of these signs in yourself or someone else, call emergency services immediately. Do not wait to see if symptoms improve, and do not attempt to drive the person to hospital yourself — paramedics can begin assessment and, in some cases, treatment on the way to hospital, and hospitals need advance notice to prepare their stroke team.

## Step 2: While Waiting for the Ambulance

- Note the time symptoms started, if known — this is critical information for the treatment team, as some treatments are only effective within a specific time window after symptom onset.
- Keep the person calm, safe, and comfortable. Loosen any tight clothing.
- Do not give the person anything to eat or drink, as swallowing may be impaired and this creates a choking risk.
- Do not give aspirin or other medication, since some strokes involve bleeding in the brain, which certain medicines can worsen.
- Monitor breathing and responsiveness until help arrives.

## Step 3: Treatment in the First 24 Hours

Once at the hospital, doctors will quickly assess what type of stroke has occurred, usually with a brain scan, because treatment depends on whether the stroke is caused by a blood clot (ischemic stroke, the most common type) or bleeding (hemorrhagic stroke). Depending on the type and how soon the patient arrives, treatment in the first 24 hours may include:

- **Thrombolysis** — "clot-busting" medicine given to dissolve a blood clot blocking blood flow to the brain. This is time-critical and most effective when given as soon as possible after symptoms start.
- **Thrombectomy** — a procedure to physically remove a blood clot from a blood vessel in the brain, sometimes possible for certain large-vessel blockages.
- **Pressure-relief procedures** — for some hemorrhagic strokes, surgery may be needed to stop pressure building up inside the skull or brain, or to drain fluid.

A healthcare team of doctors, specialists, and therapists works together from admission to begin stabilizing the patient and starting the recovery process, which may include physiotherapy, speech and language therapy, and occupational therapy in the following days and weeks.

## Why Speed Matters

The phrase used by stroke specialists is often summarized as "time is brain" — because brain tissue dies rapidly once blood supply is interrupted, the sooner treatment starts, the more brain function can potentially be saved and the better the chances of a fuller recovery. This is true even if symptoms seem to improve or disappear on their own; a brief episode that resolves may be a transient ischemic attack (TIA), which is a serious warning sign that a full stroke could follow soon and still requires urgent medical assessment the same day.
