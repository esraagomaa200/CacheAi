# Emergency Numbers in Egypt — Ambulance and How to Call

Knowing which number to call, and what to say, can save critical minutes in a medical emergency in Egypt.

## The Main Numbers

- **123 — Ambulance.** This is the direct emergency number for the Egyptian Ambulance Authority (EAA), which operates under the Ministry of Health and Population. Ambulance service through this number is free of charge and available 24 hours a day, nationwide, covering both first aid on scene and transport of emergency cases to the nearest appropriate hospital.
- **112 — Unified emergency number.** Egypt also operates a unified national emergency hotline that can connect callers to police, ambulance, and fire services from a single number, useful if you are unsure which specific service you need or if 123 is not reachable.
- **16474 — Ministry of Health public helpline.** This line handles general public health inquiries and guidance, separate from dispatching an ambulance for an active emergency.

## What to Do When You Call

1. **Stay calm and speak clearly.** Give your exact location first — street name, nearest landmark, building number, floor, and city or governorate. In Egypt, landmarks (a well-known shop, mosque, or intersection) are often more useful to responders than an exact address.
2. **Describe the emergency briefly and clearly** — what happened, how many people are affected, and the patient's current condition (for example: conscious or not, breathing or not, bleeding, chest pain, or stroke-like symptoms).
3. **Follow the dispatcher's instructions.** They may guide you through first aid steps, such as CPR instructions, while the ambulance is on its way.
4. **Do not hang up until told to.** Keep your phone line free and accessible in case the dispatcher or ambulance team needs to call back for directions.
5. **If possible, send someone outside** to wait at the street entrance or a visible landmark to flag down and guide the ambulance, especially in areas with unclear addressing, informal housing, or heavy traffic — this can save valuable minutes.
6. **Have identifying information ready if available** — the patient's name, age, known chronic conditions, current medications, and any known allergies. This helps paramedics and the receiving hospital act faster and more safely.

## After the Call

Keep the patient safe, calm, and, unless trained to do otherwise, in the position they are most comfortable in while waiting — except in specific situations where a condition (like suspected stroke, choking, or cardiac arrest) already requires a particular position or immediate action, as advised elsewhere in this guidance. Do not attempt to transport a seriously ill or injured patient yourself if an ambulance is on the way, since paramedics can begin treatment during transport and hospitals can prepare in advance once notified.

## Why This Matters

For time-critical emergencies such as stroke, heart attack, choking, and severe asthma attacks, getting professional help started as early as possible — even before arrival at a hospital — has a direct impact on outcomes. Saving the number 123 in your phone, and making sure family members and caregivers (especially those caring for elderly relatives or people with chronic conditions) know it too, is one of the simplest and most effective things anyone can do to prepare for a medical emergency in Egypt.
